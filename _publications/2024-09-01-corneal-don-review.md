The purpose of this study was to review available literature that discusses the attitudes, awareness, and perceptions among individuals and populations regarding corneal and eye donation worldwide, to identify barriers that hinder donor programs from achieving sustainable success.
A search was performed in PubMed yielding 800 articles which resulted in a final set of 101 relevant articles for review. Articles were excluded after abstract review if the authors determined the articles did not sufficiently pertain to corneal donation. Information was extracted regarding year of publication, country of origin, income level, World Bank region, study aim, study setting, sample size, and percentage of consented donors. Qualitative information was extracted on barriers to corneal donation and discussions or mentions of eye banks.
Of the 101 studies, 34% were in South Asia, 26% were in Europe and Central Asia, and 22% were in East Asia and Pacific. Fifty-six percent of the studies were published in upper-middle to high-income countries. Barriers to corneal donation were a common focus of the studies, and discussions of eye banks or eye banking systems appeared in 75% of the data set. Barriers to corneal donation in these studies were categorized as stemming from perceptions and awareness, family and religious factors, and societal factors.
The unique nature of eye donation emerged as a significant factor affecting willingness to donate. The interactions with trained donation coordinators are essential for the success of eye banking service delivery, and eye banks remain the proven system for ensuring the availability of corneal tissue for transplant.
